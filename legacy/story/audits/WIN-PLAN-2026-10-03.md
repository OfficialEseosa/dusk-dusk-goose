# Plan to win: Last Night on Maple Street

Written 2026-10-03 from four research reports plus the audit in `AUDIT-2026-10-03-0127.md`. Entry deadline: **October 30, 2026, 11:59 PM Pacific** (rules PDF; the public page says the 31st, plan for the 30th). 27 days.

Claims are marked **[verified]** (read from a source), **[third-party]** (reported by others, not confirmed from OpenAI), or **[judgement]** (my recommendation).

## 1. The finding that changes everything

**The game as built cannot be published on ChatGPT Sites.** [third-party]

- The publishing feature is called ChatGPT Sites, inside ChatGPT Work mode. It runs on a serverless platform with a small built-in database and file storage.
- It is reported **not** to support Node servers, always-on processes, or WebSocket servers. The current game is exactly that: one always-on Node server holding rooms in memory with a live socket per player.
- OpenAI's own help page was blocked to the researcher, so this comes from two third-party write-ups. **ChatGPT itself can confirm it in one question** (Prompt 0 below).
- One other entrant has already published a multiplayer game on Sites. Their approach: the server keeps each room in the built-in database, and each phone asks for updates a few times a second instead of holding a live connection. [verified from that entrant's public repo]

Consequence: the networking has to be rebuilt for Sites regardless of anything else. Since you also want 3D and stick controls, the screen side is being rebuilt too. **Treat this as a restart that keeps the story, the rules, and the lessons, not as a repair job.** The server rule book (`server/game.ts`) and the story lines (`server/story.ts`) are worth carrying over as the description of how the game behaves.

What "updates a few times a second" means for design: your friend's flashlight and position will arrive in small steps and be smoothed on screen. That suits a slow, atmospheric game. It does not suit anything needing split-second timing, so the goodbye signal needs a generous window.

## 2. What the contest actually asks [verified from the rules PDF and mission page]

- A live multiplayer game at its own public URL, joined with a room code, no logins, no installs, screens in sync, replayable, from any phone or laptop.
- Requirement to take part: a ChatGPT account with Work mode.
- Submit: title, cover image, description, link.
- Four criteria at 25% each, scored 1 to 5. The top marks read: "fully functional, stable, and demo-ready end-to-end"; "fresh concept, unexpected use case, or particularly clever implementation"; "delights its intended audience"; "feels intentional and real, with thoughtful details, edge cases, and clear guidance".
- Round 1 scores every entry; the top 20 go to a panel; top three get $1,000 each.
- The rules PDF has **no clause about outside tools or assets**. It refers to a Code of Conduct the researcher could not retrieve.

**Not known:** how judges test, and whether the signed-in mission page restricts outside assets or recommends a way to do multiplayer. Only you can read that page.

## 3. Recommended decisions [judgement]

| Decision | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | **One polished chapter of 4 to 5 minutes** with a real ending, not three acts | Judges spend minutes. Three of four criteria are scored on what they touch. |
| Look | **Real 3D, low-poly, fixed camera angle per room** (a diorama), built from free asset kits | The flashlight becomes a real light; darkness hides roughness; kits give one consistent style without an artist. |
| Controls | **Left stick to walk, right side action buttons that appear near an object**, plus a radio button | Your idea. Standard on phones, and removes all page scrolling. |
| Camera | Fixed angle that frames the room; the character's facing aims the flashlight | One stick is enough. First-person needs a second look control and shows every collision bug. |
| Screen | Landscape, fills the screen, never scrolls. Fullscreen button on Android and desktop. iPhone gets an "Add to Home Screen" tip because iPhone Safari cannot fullscreen a web page [verified] | Fixes the two things you disliked most. |
| Solo judging | Two tabs in one window must work, and neither tab may freeze the other | Most likely way a judge tests. |
| Sound | Crickets, radio squelch, flashlight click, one stinger for the figure, a short motif at the goodbye | Cheapest polish there is. |

Fallbacks, in order, if something proves too hard: drop walking and keep the same 3D rooms with tap-to-interact; if asset files cannot be used, have ChatGPT build the rooms from simple 3D shapes in the same style.

### The chapter [judgement]

1. Join by code, pick a house. One tap starts the night and turns on sound.
2. Lit bedroom, then the power cuts on both screens together. Real darkness.
3. Walk to your flashlight. Pick it up with the action button.
4. Walk to the window. Your beam appears in your friend's room, and theirs in yours. This is the "wow" and should land by about 45 seconds of play.
5. One puzzle that truly needs talking: your friend's light reveals something only you can see; you describe it; only they can act on it. Unlabelled objects, many possible answers, different each room, a short flicker penalty for a wrong try.
6. The figure with the box crosses the street. One player's room has half-packed boxes they would rather not light.
7. The reveal in one object and one line. Then the two-flash goodbye, held together with a forgiving window.
8. End card, with Swap Houses and Play Again.

### Design rules worth keeping from the research

- Never show a dead "waiting" screen; the idle player always has something to light or find.
- Always show whether your friend is there (a small radio light), and reconnect without drama.
- Both players should have spoken within 90 seconds.
- Nothing essential depends on hearing, on colour alone, or on vibration (iPhone Safari has no vibration [verified]).

## 4. Tools and assets

**Free asset kits, no credit required (CC0):**
- Kenney: City Kit (Suburban), Furniture Kit, Car Kit, Building Kit, Blocky Characters. [verified]
- KayKit: Furniture Bits, City Builder Bits. [verified]
- Quaternius: house interior, furniture, streets, characters. Licence confirmed second-hand only.
- No ready water tower was found. Not needed if the chapter ends on the street.

**AI model generators:** Meshy's free tier gives roughly three textured models a month and requires credit [verified]. No evidence ChatGPT can generate 3D models itself. Use generators only for a single hero prop, if at all.

**ChatGPT image generation** is useful for the cover image, posters on the bedroom wall, and UI art.

**Sound:** a free loopable crickets track on OpenGameArt and free night recordings on BigSoundBank [verified]. Radio static still to find (search Freesound, CC0 filter).

**Phone limits (rules of thumb, not official):** keep each scene light (well under 100,000 triangles), one or two shadow-casting lights, and test on a real mid-range Android in week one.

**Not confirmed:** whether Sites accepts 3D model files and what its size limits are. Prompt 0 asks.

## 5. How to use ChatGPT better

From OpenAI's own guidance as summarized by the researcher [third-party summaries of OpenAI pages]:

1. **It builds what the words say.** The current spec says "skeleton", "graybox", "simple temporary artwork". It delivered exactly that. Remove those words.
2. **Short and consistent beats long and thorough.** Conflicting rules hurt more than missing ones. One page of standing rules, one plan, each rule stated once. Retire `GAME_SPEC.md` and the old brief as instructions.
3. **Describe the finished result and how to tell it is done**, not the steps.
4. **Show it the target.** Have it produce a mood board (three images) and approve one before it builds.
5. **Make it look at its own work** at phone size before it says done.
6. **One slice at final quality before any breadth.**
7. **One piece of work per chat; give feedback as what you experienced** ("text unreadable on my phone", "the page scrolls"), ideally with a screenshot.

### Standing rules for ChatGPT (paste once, keep as the project's instruction file)

```
PROJECT: Last Night on Maple Street. A two-player co-op story game for phones,
published on ChatGPT Sites for the Handshake x OpenAI multiplayer game challenge.

WHAT IT IS: Two best friends on the last night of summer 2002. The power goes out.
Each player is in their own house across the street, with a walkie-talkie and a
flashlight. Each player's flashlight beam is visible in the other player's room.
They need each other's light and descriptions to get through one short chapter
that ends with a goodbye signal, because one of them is moving away in the morning.

QUALITY BAR: This is a finished, shipped game, not a prototype. It should look
and feel like a small commercial mobile game.

ALWAYS TRUE:
- Two players join from separate devices with a room code. No login, no install.
- The server decides everything that both players share. Players' screens agree.
- Runs entirely on ChatGPT Sites at a public URL.
- Real 3D scenes, dark after the power cut, lit by the players' flashlights.
- Landscape. The game fills the screen. The page never scrolls or zooms.
- Phone controls: move stick on the left; action buttons on the right that appear
  only when the player is near something usable; a radio button always reachable.
- Keyboard and mouse work on a laptop.
- Text is at least 16 pixels on a small phone. Touch targets at least 44 pixels.
- A hidden tab or a slow connection never freezes the other player.
- A player who refreshes, locks their phone, or reopens the link in the same
  browser returns to the same seat and the same moment.
- The words prototype, graybox, placeholder and temporary never appear on screen.

DONE MEANS: before you tell me something is finished, open the game yourself at a
small landscape phone size and at laptop size, look at it, and confirm: nothing
is cut off, nothing scrolls, every control is reachable with thumbs, and two
separate browser sessions can play through what you built. Tell me exactly what
you checked and what you could not check.

HOW WE WORK: one change per request. Before changing anything, tell me your plan
in two or three sentences. If something I ask for is not possible on ChatGPT
Sites, say so plainly and offer the nearest thing that is. Do not add features I
did not ask for.
```

### Prompt sequence

```
PROMPT 0: Tell me what ChatGPT Sites can do (no building yet)

I am building a two-player real-time browser game to publish on ChatGPT Sites.
Before any work, answer these plainly. Do not build or change anything.

1. Can a Site keep a live two-way connection open to each player (WebSockets or
   similar), or must each device ask the server for updates repeatedly?
2. Where can a Site keep shared room state between requests, and how quickly can
   two devices see each other's changes?
3. Can a Site serve 3D model files, images and sound files that I upload? What
   are the size limits per file and in total?
4. Can a Site use the Three.js library and a virtual joystick library?
5. Can you open the published Site yourself at phone size and look at it, or take
   screenshots of it?
6. Is there anything in the "Create a Multiplayer Game" mission instructions about
   how multiplayer should be built, or about using outside asset packs?
7. Roughly how much of my plan's usage allowance does publishing and running a
   small game consume?

For each answer, say whether you are certain or guessing.
```
