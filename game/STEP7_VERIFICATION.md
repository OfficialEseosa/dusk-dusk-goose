## Radio and persistent controls

Radio is available throughout hiding, preparation/waiting, seeking, reveal and final scores. Phone: the right button sits above the action. Laptop: R toggles it and Escape closes it, including while its input has focus. Opening does not autofocus the text box, so keyboard movement remains available until the player explicitly selects the field. Four phrases are selected using only public role and phase. Seekers can point out footprints, bins, nearby help or splitting up; hiders tease without any derived location; waiting players can say Ready when you are. Reveal/final phrases fit the result/replay.

The server accepts one to 80 Unicode code points, rejects empty/control-character text, and trims surrounding whitespace. HTML-looking text stays literal. Sender ID/name, message kind, identifier and timestamp are server-assigned; supplied sender/kind fields are ignored. No position, hiding spot, mark/trail identity, secret seed or private clue offer is attached to a radio event. Quick phrases never interpolate private state. Player-authored descriptions are ordinary chat, not automatic secret disclosures.

Each seat may send three messages in a sliding five-second window, preserved through credential takeover. Radio bypasses movement and gameplay request allowances. An accepted message fans out as a small event; its result contains that message, rather than a new full trace snapshot. Rejected floods receive at most a ten-per-second replenishing reply allowance with a ten-reply burst. Beyond that replies are dropped, bounding denial traffic. Normal UI keeps one send in flight and shows friendly validation/rate/reconnection notes. Rooms store only 24 history entries, including scheduled clues marked Clue; history restores after refresh and resets on replay or a roster-change fresh match. Round changes retain it.

The input, send button, four phrase buttons, 24 history row containers, radio toggle, stick, action and game view are created once. Snapshots update their contents/state. Incoming events merge by identifier and never replace the focused input, clear its draft or close the panel. Typing fields clear movement input and inhibit movement/action/radio hotkeys; entering a field also cancels an existing search hold. Deliberate stick use blurs the text field. A compact keyboard layout keeps composition visible under reduced viewport height without document scrolling, and hides the room code so the phase/countdown remains readable.

The panel leaves the world and thumb controls around it visible. History has its own bounded scrolling region; the page does not scroll. Player labels reserve the open panel/button rectangles. The radio button shifts upward if its ordinary position intersects the own-character screen bounds. Score screens use a top button and place brief transmission captions within their persistent score panel, where they cannot be covered by it. Scoreboard captions are one line with an ellipsis; full text remains in history and the status announcement.
