# Finishing verification

October 4, 2026. Step 7 was independently verified before this pass. No deployment, new artwork, street layouts, scoring changes or additional gameplay features.

## What changed

The landscape phone radio is a 132-pixel strip at y=52 at 667x375, ending at y=184 within the top half. Compose, Send and Close share the first row. Four phrases occupy a horizontally scrollable 44-pixel row. Two single-line recent messages occupy the last two rows, with their own bounded scrolling history. Laptop keeps the larger history panel. Validation notes fit inside the strip; successful Sent feedback clears after 1.5 seconds.

The raised camera's lookahead can put the character near the top when facing east or south. Instead of changing camera scale or the shared beam envelope, the dock occupies the wider side of the top edge whenever its vertical band intersects the projected character. Its width and position change in place; sizing is clamped to the viewport and body avoidance is disabled behind score overlays, so the reveal camera cannot create an oversized strip. The input, draft, focus, stick and action nodes keep their identity. The phone panel was clear of the character in all four directions, with about 49-pixel characters. The bottom instruction stays visible. At reduced keyboard viewport height the game header and duplicate radio toggle hide; countdown, compose/close, phrase row and recent history stay available, and thumb controls become smaller while remaining above the 44-pixel target minimum.

Fullscreen appears only if the browser reports fullscreen enabled and supplies requestFullscreen. Enter/exit operates on the document without rebuilding the street. A failed request leaves play available and offers Try fullscreen. Unsupported browsers instead receive a dismissible Share/Add to Home Screen tip; dismissal is remembered locally. Portrait adds a non-blocking turn-sideways hint only in play, never on the title or lobby.

The title says: Dark hide-and-seek. 1 to 6 players. Each round shows a persistent five-second role card: burial for a human hider, finding with light-only evidence for seekers, or a next-round/pickup instruction for waiting players. Its clock starts after the scene loads so asset loading does not consume the card. It fades without capturing movement or clicks. A hider refreshing after burial receives the appropriate trail/light instruction rather than being told to bury an already hidden capsule. First usable action receives a brief border pulse, remembered on the device; reduced-motion preference uses a static highlight. No tutorial screen was added.

A source audit found no visible test labels, developer/prototype wording, unfinished labels or em dashes. Existing canvas diagnostic datasets remain invisible and contain public/local scene observations; they are not UI text or public debug routes. Teaching/display rectangles are reserved during name-label placement. Reconnection/error status was moved above the ordinary bottom instruction.

The last test ran both human and solo clue schedules at their real 30/60/90-second times and checked trails, three decoys, light-only visibility and freeze. Evidence: design/finishing-browser-tests.txt. Targeted checks first caught phone height/keyboard conflicts, a scoreboard header overlap and reveal-camera sizing; these were corrected before the full passing run. The final run measured radio arrival at 58 ms, accepted movement from the other player during a radio flood after 379 ms (47.8 FPS during that sample), silent-drop recovery in 873 ms with the stale upstream still open, remote movement in 153 ms and blackout skew of 2 ms. These are desktop Chromium measurements, not mobile-network or phone-performance claims.

Independent Chromium sessions use touch-enabled 667x375 and 1366x768. Real Chromium requestFullscreen and exitFullscreen succeeded. A test-only capability override reported fullscreen unsupported: no button appeared, the home-screen tip appeared, Close dismissed it, and refresh retained dismissal. A 390x844 portrait check found no orientation hint on title/lobby and a hint during play. Title description, both human roles and the solo seeker role were checked. Role cards faded after five seconds, did not replay on snapshots, and first usable action received attention.

Four-direction dock checks measured no intersection with the projected character: north full-width 651 pixels; east 481 pixels on the right; south 376 pixels on the left; west 481 pixels on the left. All strips ended at y=184. Horizontal phrase scrolling reached and sent the initially clipped last phrase. At 667x235, composition retained focus/draft, the countdown remained readable, visible buttons stayed in bounds with at least 44-pixel targets and 16-pixel text, and the page did not scroll. This reduced viewport simulates space left by a keyboard; it does not emulate an actual Android or iOS keyboard. The compact strip remains in the upper 158 pixels of the original phone display, rather than claiming it fits inside half of the reduced 235-pixel viewport.

## Limits

Actual Android/iPhone fullscreen behavior, standalone Add to Home Screen, safe-area/browser-chrome interactions, OS keyboards, physical thumb comfort, mobile audio interruptions, cellular switching and sustained phone frame rate remain real-device checks. Chromium's unavailable-fullscreen capability test is not a Safari run. Browser audio regression checks measure synthesized output; phone speaker quality has not been auditioned. Local clean-copy verification does not establish Railway Linux/container/TLS behavior or owner account settings. Room capacity/load, physical phone heat/battery and mobile bandwidth are not newly load-tested. The existing Vite bundle-size advisory remains; no new artwork or scoring tuning was attempted.
